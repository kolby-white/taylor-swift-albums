Taylor Swift released her first album at 16, as a country singer on an independent Nashville label. Twelve albums later she has moved through country, pop, indie folk and synth-pop, won Album of the Year four times (more than any other artist), and taken back ownership of the recordings she lost in 2019. This story follows that arc album by album, then maps where it happened.

The albums

How the music evolved

Swift has changed genre more often than almost any major artist, but her songwriting has stayed recognizable throughout. These charts trace both. The ratings are this story's editorial reading of each album as a whole; individual songs vary.

2006–2010: Nashville

Acoustic guitar, fiddle and banjo with pop-sized choruses. Even the first album was country pop, built for radio.

2012–2017: Into pop

Red was the hinge, with banjo next to dubstep. By 1989 the guitars were gone, and Reputation pushed into hard electronic beats.

2020: The left turn

Folklore and Evermore traded synths for piano, strings and the muted indie rock of The National's Aaron Dessner.

2022–2025: Synthesis

Midnights and The Tortured Poets Department combine the synth-pop and the hushed folk palettes. The Life of a Showgirl returns to bright, big-chorus pop.

What the songs are about

CentralPresent

Love is the one constant: it is central or present on every album. Heartbreak drives the early records and the 2020 pair, but is absent from Reputation and The Life of a Showgirl, both written early in new relationships. Growing up fades after Speak Now. Fame becomes a subject with 1989 and Reputation and returns on every album except the 2020 sister records. With Folklore she starts writing about invented people.

Songwriting style

Her own framework: the three pens

In a 2022 speech to the Nashville Songwriter Awards, Swift sorted her lyrics into three types, each named after a pen. The example songs are this story's picks.

  • Quill: archaic, ornate language, as if written by a 19th-century poet. For example, "ivy" or "The Lakes".
  • Fountain pen: modern, personal and vividly specific. For example, "All Too Well".
  • Glitter gel pen: frivolous and bouncy, not taking itself seriously. For example, "Shake It Off" or "22".

Signatures that run through every era

  • The bridge. Her bridges often turn the song or raise its intensity, as in "Cruel Summer", "Out of the Woods" and "Death by a Thousand Cuts".
  • Specific detail. A scarf left at a sister's house, a refrigerator light, a street name. Small objects carry the emotion.
  • Track 5. Fans treat the fifth track as the album's most vulnerable song ("All Too Well", "Dear John", "my tears ricochet"), and Swift has acknowledged the pattern.
  • Easter eggs. Hidden capital-letter messages in early lyric booklets grew into clues spread across videos, outfits and social media.

    Who owns the music? The re-recordings

    When Swift left Big Machine Records in 2018, the label kept the masters, the original recordings of her first six albums. In June 2019 Scooter Braun's Ithaca Holdings bought Big Machine, and those masters with it. Swift publicly objected and said she had not been given a fair chance to buy them herself.

    Because she still held the songwriting (publishing) rights, she could legally record the songs again. New recordings meant new masters that she owned, and she could license those to films, ads and streaming in place of the originals. Each one was labelled "(Taylor's Version)" and included unreleased "From the Vault" tracks.

    Four albums were re-recorded between 2021 and 2023. Then in May 2025 Swift announced that she had bought back the original masters of all six albums, so the plan to re-record Taylor Swift and Reputation was no longer needed.

    Source excerpt (Wikipedia)

    "In June 2019, a dispute emerged between American singer-songwriter Taylor Swift and her former record label, Big Machine Records, its founder Scott Borchetta, and its new owner Scooter Braun over the ownership of the masters of her first six studio albums. The private equity firm Shamrock Holdings acquired the masters in 2020, whereupon Swift re-recorded and released four of the albums from 2021 to 2023 to exert control over her music catalog."

    "By re-recording, Swift is technically covering her own songs as new recordings, resulting in new masters she fully owns, enabling her to control the licensing of her songs for commercial use, known as synchronization, by evading the owners of the older masters and subsequently devaluing them."

    Source excerpt (Wikipedia)

    "Spanning 149 shows in 51 cities across 21 countries and five continents, the Eras Tour had a large cultural and socioeconomic impact. It became the highest-grossing tour of all time and the first to earn over $1 billion and $2 billion in revenue."

    "Designed as a tribute to Swift's discography across her career since 2006, the Eras Tour covered all of the styles of music seen in her 11 studio albums, including The Tortured Poets Department, which was added in the European leg of the tour after the album's release... According to the music journalist Alexis Petridis, the Eras Tour was a musically eclectic production, consisting of 'dubstep-inspired, dark-hued pop; tweedy folk; monster-chorus-sporting anthems and acoustic guitar-driven songs that show her Nashville grounding'."

    "A typical show of the Eras Tour lasted 3 hours and 15 minutes. It consisted of between 44 and 46 songs, grouped into 10 acts, representing each 'musical era' of her discography. Each act was characterized by a specific color scheme, costume, and stage design..."

    On screen

    The tour was filmed and released three times.

    Latest: The Life of a Showgirl: The Encore (2026)

    In September 2026 Swift added four tracks to The Life of a Showgirl as a deluxe edition. She revealed the bonus single "Patient Zero" on social media on September 22, confirmed the edition the next day, and released the track on September 25.

    "The story behind me writing the song 'Patient Zero' is that I was hanging out with a bunch of friends, and one of my friends told us that she had been reached out to by the new girlfriend of an ex of hers, asking for guidance on if she had had the same experiences with this guy."Taylor Swift, interview with Capital FM

    Fan theory. Some fans guessed the song was written for a film, as "I Knew It, I Knew You" was for Toy Story 5. After Swift was seen with Tom Cruise at a Chiefs game, they linked it to his film Digger. (via MSN)
